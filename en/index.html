The Sunday feature 22 November

Research on AI and learning

AI helps learning more when it is used to understand than when it does the task

A review by Stanford University now brings together 52 studies measuring the effect of AI on learning: using it to understand new material fares better than using it to produce the work, although no use improves results consistently. This report explains how to read those studies, why a tutor that asks questions is not enough on its own and what a teacher can do so that AI does not do the students’ work.

Tools

Friday, 27 NovemberSeptember announcement for Claude

Claude’s projects go from folder to conversation that splits the work into threads

In September Anthropic announced a redesign of Claude’s projects, in beta and for Claude Code: users describe what they need and Claude divides it into tasks that run in the cloud.

Sunday, 22 NovemberAutomating notebooks

Gemini Notebook now has an official API, though limited and in preview

It lets other programs create notebooks and add sources to them, but only with personal accounts. An unofficial code library does more, although it may stop working without warning.

Thursday, 19 NovemberStanford study

Stanford gathers 52 causal studies on AI in schools and sees uneven results

The November review goes from 20 to 52 studies and concludes that AI looks more promising for understanding new material than for producing assignments, but no use improves learning consistently.

Vibe Coding Responsable 4.0: anything published openly must be reusable

Version 4.0 of Juan José de Haro’s guide explains that the educational exception in copyright law does not cover what is published on an open website.

Multimedia

Friday, 27 NovemberFrom the EduMedia archive

HeyGen clones a voice for avatar videos, and its free plan allows three a month

The tool turns a text into audio or into a video with a presenter, using a voice cloned from a short recording. Only an adult can use it, and only with the consent of the person whose voice it is.

Thursday, 26 NovemberFrom the EduMedia archive

Ideogram lets you repeat the same character across several images for classroom materials

The image tool includes a free plan with weekly credits. On that plan, images are public, and students who are minors need their family’s permission.

Wednesday, 25 NovemberConversation in the group

Connecting AI to Blender or Unreal uses up the usage limit very quickly, according to EduMedia-IAG

According to the group, connecting coding assistants to 3D and video creation programs sends consumption soaring, although it works out cheaper than learning those programs.

Debates

Monday, 23 NovemberAuthorship and assessment

An imagined case asks who signs a prize-winning story illustrated with AI

An instalment of the series “Familias con inteligencia” imagines a case: a student declares that they used AI, wins a competition and a classmate challenges it. Chema Falcó asks schools for clear criteria.

Friday, 20 NovemberA debate from the nineties

The 1994 Clark–Kozma debate helps in reading Stanford’s studies on AI

An academic argument over whether technology influences learning or only the method matters, recalled in the group in connection with Stanford’s review.

Friday, 20 NovemberCommunity resource

A teacher reflective journal with AI raises the question of whether the assistant supports or replaces judgement

A master’s thesis project proposes dictating after each class and having an assistant organise the text and return questions; the group debates whether that weakens judgement.

Friday, 27 NovemberCompany report

A Coursera report measures demand for AI and human-skills courses

The Global Skills Report 2026, published in September, uses data from the Coursera and Udemy platforms and presents an index combining learning activity with each country’s readiness for AI.

Wednesday, 25 NovemberAI training for teachers

Microsoft brings Elevate for Educators, AI training for teachers, to Spain

The platform brings together courses, live seminars and a community of teachers, with three levels of recognition for teachers and another three for schools.

Navarre plans to use AI in school inspection without saying which programs or what data

The inspectorate’s action plan for the 2026-2027 school year includes AI as a support tool but does not specify tools or data safeguards, according to OKDIARIO.

Thursday, 26 NovemberOECD and EU framework

OECD and EU set out four areas of AI literacy for primary and secondary schools

The non-binding AILit framework describes in 19 competences, with three levels of progression and classroom examples, what students should know, do and value when it comes to artificial intelligence.

Thursday, 26 NovemberEU guidelines

Eight EU criteria for choosing and evaluating digital educational content in the classroom

Sunday, 22 NovemberHandbook for teachers

An Argentine handbook brings the environmental, labour and social costs of AI into the classroom

Two researchers propose that secondary school teachers ask what for, from where and with what consequences artificial intelligence is used, before deciding how. The 2026 handbook takes a Latin American and feminist perspective.
