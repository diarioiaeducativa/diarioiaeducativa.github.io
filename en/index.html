The Sunday feature 15 November

Assessment and AI

AI-text detectors are not proof, and institutions propose assessing the process instead

Programs that promise to reveal whether a piece of work was written by artificial intelligence get it wrong, including for students who have not used it, and even their own makers advise against punishing anyone on their say-so. This report explains what the studies, the European Regulation, data protection authorities and education guidelines say, and sets out a no-cost plan for assessing fairly in any subject: state which uses are allowed, ask for evidence of the process and listen to students.

Wharton releases Primer, an open system that turns a conversation into a simulation

Wharton Generative AI Labs offers an open-source prototype in which a team of agents interviews the teacher and builds a simulation for students. Its authors warn that it has not been evaluated in classrooms.

EduGems becomes EduPrompts because Google is replacing Gems with Skills

The site with 174 prompts for schools changes its name and address. The prompts can still be copied into any other AI tool.

Anthropic proposes organising AI agents' memory into four layers with a periodic review

An Anthropic research text describes how to give an agent memory between sessions, and a teacher in the group has adapted their own system to that idea and explains it in an article with instructions.

Debates

13 NovemberAssessment

England's university regulator calls for more assessment without AI and reopens the debate on how to verify authorship

The Office for Students recommends secure formats such as in-person or oral exams. In the group, members debated what can and cannot be detected.

13 NovemberWorkflows

Terminal or app: teachers compare ways of working with a coding agent

The group discussed whether the terminal offers more freedom than the graphical app, and concluded that it depends on the person and the task.

9 NovemberAI detectors

An AI detector's percentage opens a conversation with the student, but does not prove they copied

A post on the blog “Familias con inteligencia” asks what to do when a detector flags the work of a student who says they wrote it themselves, and suggests backing the percentage up with evidence of the process.

Library

13 NovemberUNESCO guide

UNESCO proposes five units for students aged 13 to 15 to question AI’s answers and decide for themselves

“AI answers. You decide” is a companion guide to UNESCO’s media and information literacy curriculum, with classroom discussions and activities that can be used separately.

13 NovemberRegulator's guidance and report

England's university regulator clarifies which AI practices fit its quality conditions and publishes the study behind them

The Office for Students and Advance HE have put out a 48-page report, based on more than 300 students and staff, and a guide that translates its findings into registration conditions B1, B2 and B4.

The ad hoc committee of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology proposes eight principles and a set of recommendations for adapting university teaching to generative AI.
