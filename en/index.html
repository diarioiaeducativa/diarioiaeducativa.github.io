The Sunday feature 8 November · 9 minute read

AI and age in the classroom

The age for using AI in class depends on three different rules: those of the companies, the law and the education authorities

Each service’s terms, data protection law and some education authorities set different ages for using artificial intelligence, and they are often confused. This report explains what each says as of 6 November 2026, why research asks us to swap the question of age for the question of purpose, and how a primary or secondary teacher can decide which tool to use, with which account, and who operates it.

Debates

7 November

Families and schools

Teachers debate whether families’ authorisation is needed to use AI with students

One school has limited generative AI with its students because some families did not authorise it; other views hold that simply informing them is enough. The rules are clear: families must be informed, but between 14 and 18 their consent is not needed unless the tool requires it.

4 November

MIT report

An MIT committee calls for rethinking aims, assessment and the in-person community in the face of an AI that can already do almost any written work

The ad hoc committee of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology proposes eight principles and a set of recommendations for adapting university teaching to generative AI.

3 November

Report published by UNESCO

A report for UNESCO proposes replacing “at what age?” with “for what purpose and on what evidence?” when using AI in the classroom

Sonia Livingstone and three co-authors argue that there is no single right age for using AI in education and call for decisions based on child development and clear educational aims.

3 November

World Economic Forum report

The World Economic Forum sets out seven practices to make AI safe for children without shutting them out of learning

A September 2026 document from the Global Coalition for Digital Safety takes children’s well-being, and not merely the absence of incidents, as its goal, and proposes practices for those who design AI and incentives to ensure they are followed.
