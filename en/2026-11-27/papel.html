Institutions & Industry Saturday, 28 November

Google Workspace update

From February 2027, Google will require AI agents to use its own tools

Agents that read or write in Gmail, Drive or Docs will have to go through Google’s own tools, which ask for confirmation before sending emails or changing permissions.

Google announced on 25 November 2026, on the Google Cloud blog, that its Workspace MCP servers and its command-line interface (CLI), which is operated by typing instructions, are now generally available. An AI agent is a program that carries out tasks on its own; MCP is a standard that lets it connect to other applications, and an MCP server is the program that gives it that access. Google’s cover Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Calendar, Chat and Tasks.

According to the announcement, under Google’s new addendum on AI tools in Workspace, agents will have to use those servers or the CLI instead of the current application programming interfaces (APIs). The technical requirement starts on 1 February 2027. Traditional applications, which are not agents, can carry on using the usual APIs. Agents will also have to identify themselves with their own credentials and with the minimum permissions.

These tools ask the user for explicit confirmation before sending an email, changing a file’s sharing permissions or making bulk edits. Tasks such as creating drafts or summarising documents can be done without asking. Google says, without giving a date, that it will add restrictions so that an agent cannot see certain messages or folders.

The announcement does not say which Workspace plans it applies to, or whether it affects education plans differently. Anyone using an agent to sort a school’s email or documents may have to adapt it before that date, and it is worth checking Google’s documentation. The paper published a feature on what to decide before giving an agent permissions.

Institutions & Industry Saturday, 28 November

Survey of US teachers

AI use among US teachers stalls at 62%, one point up on 2025

An EdWeek survey of 590 teachers, carried out from August to October 2026, shows that use, which almost doubled between 2023 and 2025, has barely grown this school year.

According to a survey by the EdWeek Research Center of 590 US teachers, representative of the whole, carried out between August and October 2026, 62% use AI tools in class at least a little this school year. In 2025 it was 61%, and in 2023, 34%. The findings were published by Education Week and reproduced by GovTech on 25 November.

As of October, 64% of teachers had received at least one training session on AI. The experts consulted point to possible causes of the stall: that teachers willing to adopt it have already done so, growing resistance to screen use in some districts, a lack of clear guidance and little time to try it out. The article presents these reasons as possible, not proven.

According to the same report, other surveys in 2026 show growth: it cites one by RAND in which use for teaching maths rose from 45% to 74% between 2025 and 2026. The data are from the US and say nothing about Spain.

Vibe coding Saturday, 28 November

From the VCE repository

Sentit gathers Catalan listening exercises with synthetic voices and printable worksheets

Aarón Fortuño has created, with Codex, an open repository of listening activities that students answer on paper.

Sentit, by Aarón Fortuño, is an open-source website under the MIT licence for practising listening comprehension in Catalan. According to its author, it has 139 validated activities, each with questions, an answer key, a transcript and an illustration. Teachers filter by year, difficulty, duration or type of activity and prepare the worksheet. Its author also made Trama, the catalogue of readings, and Plec, the dictations with audio.

Students answer on paper and reach the audio with a QR code that already carries the settings: repetitions, time to read the questions, pauses and speed. Questions can be open-ended or multiple-choice. The interface is in Catalan and Spanish, and its author plans to add more years, levels and languages, starting with Spanish.

The author explains in the group how the audio is made: the script and questions are written, a second AI agent and a person check them, the voices are generated with Gemini’s voice model, the full audio is listened to and it is put together with music and ambient sounds, with credits. Among the frequent problems he mentions mispronounced words, skipped sentences and changes in the voice’s timbre when a sentence is corrected. With Gemini’s free quota he had to group several activities into each request and cut the audio afterwards. He asks others to share their experiences with synthetic voices in Catalan.

According to the author, the site is static, has no user accounts and does not collect students’ answers; the page itself says it collects visit statistics with Umami, which each person can switch off in their browser.

Multimedia Saturday, 28 November

From the EduMedia archive

Polycam turns objects and spaces into 3D models with a phone camera

The app scans what is in front of it and produces a model that can be rotated, measured and shared. Its free plan has clear limits.

Polycam is a 3D scanning platform: you walk around an object or a room with a phone or tablet camera, and the program generates a three-dimensional model and measurements. In the group, it was shared that with LiDAR (a distance sensor found on some devices), the free mode works very well.

In class, it can be used, for example, to let technology or art students scan a model or a sculpture and study it from every angle, or to preserve digitally a fragile piece that cannot be handled.

To get started, you install the app, create an account and choose the type of capture: object, space or photographs. Then you move slowly around whatever is being scanned.

According to its pricing page, in November 2026 the free plan allows 150 images per capture, 3-minute videos, 3 AI captures and 10 photogrammetry captures (models built from photos). It exports only in GLTF format, videos carry a watermark and shared links are public. Space mode, with LiDAR, is available only on compatible iOS devices. The Basic plan costs $150 a year or $30 a month.

Its privacy notice sets no minimum age, says the service is not intended for children or for images of identifiable people, and says it may pass content on to third parties, including to train AI models. That is why it is best used by teachers, scanning only objects or spaces with no students in them.

A year ago Friday, 28 November 2025

AI apps

Google’s AI web apps stayed inside Gemini; a year later, AI Studio publishes them

In November 2025, a web app that transcribed and commented on students’ audio recordings only worked inside Gemini. A year later, Google AI Studio builds apps with their own server and publishes them at a public address.

On 28 November 2025, in Vibe Coding Educativo, a teacher who had spent two days trying to build a web app that would transcribe each student’s recorded monologue and give feedback was stuck. Gemini asked her for an API key, the route by which a program uses the model, and ended up telling her it was not possible with a free account. The group explained that in Gemini’s canvas (Canvas) it could be done, but that the app would only work in there.

On 18 March 2026, Google introduced a new way to build apps in Google AI Studio, with a database, sign-in and real-time sharing. According to the AI Studio documentation, the publish button hosts the app on Cloud Run at a public address, and the documentation on its build mode adds that the API key stays on the server. Publishing requires a project with billing, except for an initial tier, which allows two apps without a billing account and does not accept Google Workspace accounts.

In Gemini, Canvas apps can be exported to Google Sites from 19 October, although Google does not make clear whether they keep the AI, and their shared links still open only in Gemini.
