Institutions & Industry 10 November

Anthropic, models

Anthropic launches Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, the latter only for verified organisations

The company announces two versions of the same model: Fable 5.2, with safeguards and no prior verification, and Mythos 5.2, only for verified organisations.

On 9 November Anthropic announced Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, which it describes as its most capable models for ambitious, open-ended technical work. Several members of the groups shared the announcement, and one of them said it was already available in the Claude app.

According to the company, they are two versions of the same model. Fable 5.2 includes safeguards against misuse in cybersecurity and biology and, according to the announcement, is available to all users, including on the cloud services of Amazon, Google and Microsoft; in the API it is called `claude-fable-5-2`. Mythos 5.2 has fewer safeguards and is reserved for organisations that pass its verification programmes in cybersecurity or life sciences.

Anthropic states that they produce results up to 30% faster than the 5.1 versions, that they outperform Opus 5.5 in almost every capability it has evaluated and that, in initial tests, the safety classifiers block 55% less than with Fable 5.1. These are the company's own figures, with no independent verification. The standard price given is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens.

For teachers who program with agents, the practical news is that the company's most powerful model is within reach of the paid plans, although Anthropic recommends starting with Opus 5.5 and moving to Fable only when the task demands it. A trial open until the 16th was mentioned in the conversation, and Anthropic’s help page confirms it: on the Pro plan and on standard Team seats, Fable is included from 9 to 16 November 2026, up to half of the weekly usage limit; the launch offers do not apply to the free plan.

Vibe coding 10 November

Agents at home

A group thread uses coding agents to control home automation

A thread in the group collects uses of agents such as Claude Code or Antigravity to run the television, music and the Home Assistant configuration, with control of privacy as a condition.

The conversation started with a question: had anyone connected an AI agent such as Claude Code to the smart devices in their home? The person who asked said the agent can control the television, type on it, send it spoken messages and operate Spotify to play music suited to the tastes of whoever is working.

Another member explained a more advanced use: they have all their home automation (lights, blinds, air conditioning, fans, television) in Home Assistant, installed on a virtual machine on a NAS. They gave Antigravity, Google's agent environment, access to the configuration folders and control it through MCP. Where they used to program each automation by hand, they now ask the agent for them from the console, which creates and applies them, and they also ask it about the state of a device, the average temperatures in a room or an error.

As a condition, they stressed that no sensitive data, such as access keys or passwords, is shared with the model. Another person in the group said that, after reading the thread, they have set up a mini PC as a home server and that Home Assistant will be next.

These are accounts of personal use, not a verified guide; the interest for teachers lies in the fact that the same method of asking the agent for configurations works for any system managed with configuration files. On giving an agent access without losing control of the data, the paper has already covered the permissions of a console agent.

Vibe coding 10 November

Community apps

Two Catalan-language apps by David Cordones: a letters and numbers game and a reception classroom

Xifres i Lletres trains vocabulary and mental arithmetic by age; Aula d'Acollida Digital offers initial language support.

David Cordones has created Xifres i Lletres, a game in Catalan inspired by the quiz show “Countdown” (“Cifras y Letras” in Spanish). It has two rounds: forming the longest valid word from a set of letters, and reaching a target number by combining operations on a set of numbers. It offers four levels, for ages 6 to 8, 9 to 11, 12 to 15 and 16 and over, and works in the browser.

He has also published Aula d'Acollida Digital, designed as initial language support for newly arrived students. According to the group's notice it includes vocabulary with audio, a progress notebook and offline operation; the page consulted does not detail those features, so they could not be confirmed.

Both applications state that the code is under an AGPL v3 licence and the content under CC BY-SA 4.0, which allows other schools to adapt them.

Workflow

How “Buzz” was shot: Claude, Blender via MCP, a virtual reality headset and Seedance

Its director details the process behind the short film the paper covered yesterday, and warns that it is expensive in credits.

After the short film shot in virtual reality was presented, someone in the group asked for the workflow to be explained. Raül Torres, its director, summed it up in four steps: an app, created with Claude, to enter Blender with the headset and record the camera movements; connecting Claude to Blender through MCP to create sets, characters and animations; the shoot with the headset; and passing the Blender videos to Seedance 2.5 to turn them into the final scenes.

He warned that this last step is expensive because it uses a lot of credits. In his post about “Buzz” he also lists Magnific and DaVinci Resolve among the tools.

Vibe coding 10 November

Community website

A website promotes Belsetán, an endangered Aragonese variety, with games and classroom worksheets

“El aprendiz del pastor” brings together history, texts, games and teaching materials on the language of the Bielsa valley.

Sandra Fortanet has published, still a work in progress, Belsetán · El aprendiz del pastor, a website about the Aragonese variety spoken in the Bielsa valley (Huesca). It includes sections on the valley, the history of its study since 1944, oral-tradition texts and letters in verse, a daily word game, a grammar quiz and eight printable teaching worksheets with answer keys. The vocabulary section is marked “coming soon”.

The project states that it uses only forms documented in its sources, flags as a reconstruction any proposal awaiting review by a speaker, and follows the spelling of the Academia Aragonesa de la Lengua. It also offers a way for speakers and relatives from the valley to contribute.

A year ago 10 November 2025

Open models

The open model that was “checkmating” the giants: a year on, Kimi is almost three times bigger and still behind

In November 2025, people were sharing that a Chinese open-weights model, Kimi K2 Thinking, beat GPT-5 on some tests. Today Kimi K3, from the same family, is almost three times bigger, but its maker admits it does not match the best closed models.

On 10 November 2025, in ChatGPT-IA-edu, an article from WWWhatsnew was doing the rounds with a bold headline: Kimi K2 Thinking, from China’s Moonshot AI, “checkmates the giants of AI”. Newly launched, it had a trillion parameters, was released under a modified MIT licence and, according to the company’s own tests, beat GPT-5 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 at searching with tools. That an open model could measure itself against those from OpenAI, Google or Anthropic seemed a surprise.

In July 2026 Moonshot unveiled Kimi K3, with 2.8 trillion parameters and a context window of a million tokens; the weights reached Hugging Face on 27 July. But the announcement itself acknowledges that its overall performance still trails that of Claude Fable 5 and GPT 5.6 Sol.

What counts as open has also changed: K3 has its own licence, and anyone offering it as a service with more than 20 million dollars in annual revenue needs an agreement with Moonshot, according to VentureBeat. A year on, an open model measuring itself against closed ones is no longer news; catching them is not yet a reality.
