Tools 6 November

Gemini and Google Sites

Gemini lets users export their interactive apps to a Google Sites site with one click

Google has made it possible to publish an interactive app created in the Gemini app as a Google Sites site. The group discussed how useful it really is for teachers.

According to the official Google Workspace Updates blog, on 19 October 2026, anyone who has created an interactive app in the Gemini app can export it to Google Sites with one click. The app opens in a private draft site, remains interactive and can be edited, published and shared like any other site.

The feature is available on web, Android and iOS for Workspace accounts, individual subscribers and personal accounts with access to Gemini. Google says it adds no new controls for administrators: Google Sites must be turned on and users must be able to create sites, and exported sites follow the organisation’s Sites and Drive policies. The rollout covers both Rapid Release and Scheduled Release domains.

In the group, one member who tried it explained that it always generates a new site, with no way of adding the app to an existing one, and that sharing directly from the Gemini canvas was already possible. Another member saw it as a resource that brings the idea within reach of beginners without technical knowledge, although it falls short for more experienced users. These points come from members’ own tests, not from the official note, which mentions no functional limitations.

Vibe coding 6 November

Shared resource

MechSimulator brings together free mechanical engineering simulators that run in the browser

A group member highlighted this website of 2D and 3D simulations as an example of what one person can achieve.

MechSimulator is a free platform, with no installation, of interactive simulators for technical education. It covers virtual lab tests, measuring instruments, mechanics, mechanisms, strength of materials, thermal and fluid systems, electricity, workshop, calculators, basic sciences and mathematics, with more than 150 simulators.

In the group it was presented as the work of a single teacher, and the website confirms this: according to its “About” page, it was created and is reviewed by Naseel Ibnu Azeez, a mechanical engineering teacher, and it is kept free through the advertising it displays. It does not say whether AI was used to build it, and its content is in English. For technology and vocational training teachers it can serve as a bank of activities, and for those who code with AI, as a reference for what is possible.

Vibe coding 6 November

New in the repository

Fil i compte lets teachers prepare printable maths problem sheets in Catalan and Spanish, with an answer key

A bank of 252 problems lets primary and lower secondary teachers pick problems, put them in order and print the worksheet or save it as a PDF.

The resource

Name
Fil i compte
Authors
Aarón Fortuño
Licence
MIT

Open the resource · Its entry in the repository

“Fil i compte” is a web tool created by Aarón Fortuño and published under the MIT licence. According to the page, it brings together 252 maths problems, in Catalan and in Spanish, designed for the middle and upper cycles of primary school and for the start of lower secondary. The resource opens in the browser, and the page asks for no registration or account.

Teachers can filter the bank by approximate level, by difficulty (accessible, intermediate or challenging) and by content: addition and subtraction, multiplication and division, combined operations, brackets, fractions, percentages, decimals, powers, roots, direct and inverse proportion or estimation. Each topic can be marked as allowed, required or excluded. There is also a reasoning category and a “distractor data” one, with problems that include information that is not needed to solve them. Those who would rather not choose can ask for an automatic worksheet suggestion, and it is possible to search by text or by each problem’s code.

Each problem is displayed with its code, level, difficulty and content tags; for example, the one about the apples in the school vegetable garden is middle-cycle and low difficulty. Once the problems have been chosen, they are put in order and the number of pages (1, 2, 4 or 6) and of problems per page is set. The type of working space is also chosen: dotted, squared or lined. The worksheet is printed and, according to the repository’s listing, it can also be saved as a PDF. The page lets users switch between the exercises and the answer key, which is printed separately. Templates can be saved in the browser, so teachers can reuse a selection on that same computer.

In class, it can be used to quickly prepare practice sheets adapted to different groups or difficulty levels within the same classroom, or for revision tasks with the solutions to hand. The problems, the answer key and the interface are in Catalan and in Spanish. The code is published on GitHub, and the page states that it uses visit analytics without personal data, which can be switched off.

Multimedia 6 November

AI video

Google unveils Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash, which generates and edits videos of up to 15 seconds

The new version of Google’s video model extends clip length and allows clips to be refined over several turns. The announcement was shared in EduMedia-IAG.

According to Google’s blog, on 5 November 2026, Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash generates and edits videos of up to 15 seconds (10 in Omni 1.1), with a duration that can be set between 3 and 15 seconds. It lets users refine the same clip turn by turn, for example by changing the background, lighting or aspect ratio, without generating it again. It costs $0.07 per second at 720p, 30% less than the previous version, and outputs carry a SynthID watermark.

It is available in the Gemini API (Google AI Studio), in Gemini for enterprise and in Google Vids, where 15-second clips carry no extra cost. In the Gemini app, 15-second video is rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers. The group asked whether it will be usable in Europe; this could not be checked.
