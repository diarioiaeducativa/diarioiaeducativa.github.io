Debates 9 November

AI detectors

An AI detector's percentage opens a conversation with the student, but does not prove they copied

A post on the blog “Familias con inteligencia” asks what to do when a detector flags the work of a student who says they wrote it themselves, and suggests backing the percentage up with evidence of the process.

Chema Falcó published “La máquina dice que copió. Él dice que lo redactó” (The machine says he copied. He says he wrote it) on 8 November, with the subtitle “Why a percentage is not proof”. It starts from a fictional case based on real situations: a 15-year-old student whose school accuses him of using AI in a history assignment because a detector gives it an 87% probability. He says he wrote it and still has his drafts, although he admits he used a spelling and grammar checker at the end.

The post argues that a detector provides an indication, not proof, and that it can be wrong in both directions. Before reaching a conclusion, it suggests listening to the student's explanation without either accepting or dismissing it, and then checking it: an oral explanation or redoing part of the exercise helps to verify what has been learned. As evidence of the process it cites drafts, versions, dates and notes, and it recommends that families be able to meet the form tutor to find out what the percentage means and what protocol the school applies. It also warns that an unfair accusation can cause anxiety and demotivation.

The text draws on UNESCO's guidance on generative AI in education and on the European AI Act, which agree that a person must review and interpret what automated tools say. On the same subject, the paper reported the publishing sector's warning about detectors, which spoke of a “witch hunt”.

Multimedia 9 November

Film with AI

A short film is shot inside virtual reality and finished with AI

Raül Torres shares a preview of “Buzz”, a short film whose shots he directs with a virtual reality headset on sets created in Blender.

Raül Torres has shared on LinkedIn a preview of “Buzz”, his new short film. He says he builds the sets in Blender and, wearing a Meta Quest 3 headset, steps into each scene and moves the camera as if he were inside it: he crouches, leans and follows the characters, with no keyboard or mouse.

These preliminary shots serve as the basis for generating the final images with AI afterwards. According to the author, the direction, pacing and camera movements come out of that phase, and feeling the space and distance from the inside changes how staging is thought about. What has been shared is only a preview: colour correction, sound design and editing are still to come.

Vibe coding 9 November

New in the repository

A planner lets schools build the school-year calendar from the official Valencian Community one or from scratch

Calendari, created by José Luis Miralles Bono with the help of Claude, lets a school build its academic calendar in the browser, starting from the official Valencian one or from scratch.

The resource

Name
Calendari — Planificador de curso
Authors
José Luis Miralles Bono
Licence
AGPL v3

Open the resource · Its entry in the repository

Calendari — Planificador de curso is a web application created by José Luis Miralles Bono, who presents it as designed with the help of Claude. According to its entry in the repository, it is intended for the school and academic calendar of a music and dance conservatoire, and is classified as a creation tool for teacher training and for teaching organisation.

On opening it, the page offers four routes: start with the official calendar of the Valencian Community, which generates the school year with the public holidays and breaks according to the municipality and the type of course; create a blank calendar, defining the school year, holidays, terms and profiles; import one previously saved as a JSON file; or browse the published calendars, which can be subscribed to or copied into one's own editor. The entry adds that it manages teaching days, terms and assessment milestones.

For a leadership team or a department, its usefulness lies in starting from a calendar that is already built and adjusting it, rather than putting one together every September. According to the entry, the result can be exported to PDF and .ics, and can be subscribed to from a mobile phone's calendar, so that teachers see the changes in their own diary.

It is used in the browser, with no account or payment requested on the home screen, and the interface is in Spanish and Valencian, with a language selector and a dark mode. According to the entry, a school can have its own copy. The source code is on GitHub under an AGPL v3 licence, and the content under CC BY-SA 4.0.

The same author has already appeared in the Diario with a calculator that splits the working week of peripatetic teachers across several schools of the Generalitat Valenciana.

A year ago 9 November 2025

Apps with memory

A word cloud with a “backend” in a single prompt: today Claude’s new artifacts save data without being published

In November 2025, a word cloud built with Claude stored everyone’s votes from a single prompt. Today the new artifacts save data without needing to be published.

On 9 November 2025, in Vibe Coding Educativo, an app built with Claude was being tested: a cloud of the countries from which members followed the group, which was meant to show everyone’s votes in real time. The group celebrated getting a “backend” from one prompt, and the questions came quickly: where was the data stored, and how could people be stopped from voting more than once? When asked, Claude itself spoke of an internal store, window.storage, with 5 MB per key.

Today Anthropic’s official help page on artifacts documents that storage. Whoever builds the app decides which data is personal, private to each user, and which is shared, visible to everyone; the first time someone uses the shared kind, Claude warns them. In new artifacts, the limit is 100 MB and 25,000 records per artifact; in those created before 16 September 2026, 20 MB and text only. It is available on paid plans. Publishing has also changed: in the older ones, storage only works once they are published, and unpublishing them deletes the data; new ones save without being published.

These apps can also call Claude: each user signs in with their own account and the usage is charged to their plan, not to that of whoever created the app.
