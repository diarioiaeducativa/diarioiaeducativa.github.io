Debates 7 November

Families and schools

Teachers debate whether families’ authorisation is needed to use AI with students

One school has limited generative AI with its students because some families did not authorise it; other views hold that simply informing them is enough. The rules are clear: families must be informed, but between 14 and 18 their consent is not needed unless the tool requires it.

A conversation in ChatGPT-IA-edu reopened a common question in schools: whether using AI with students requires families’ permission. Several positions were put forward.

The first is caution. If even one family in a class group does not give authorisation, the tool should by default not be used with that group, so as not to create differences between students. In a school that has applied it this way, generative AI is not enabled for students and AI literacy is taught without using it, while teachers train during the school year and the staff meeting is expected to decide its strategy at the end.

The second holds that, when the tool is authorised by the education authority (for example Gemini or Copilot), there is no need to ask for permission, just as none is asked for using a platform such as Moodle. It would be enough to inform families, preferably with a published record, and what would matter is the backing of the School Council, the head teacher and the staff.

A third contribution pointed out that the XTEC page on minimum ages by platform, updated on 14 July 2026, says that most platforms ask for parental authorisation between 14 and 18, so in practice it has to be requested. In reply, the guidance from the Departament d’Educació, which the paper reported on 3 November, was cited. That reading was welcomed in the group as good news because of the leeway it gives schools, although, as was acknowledged, it does not end the argument in staff meetings. The question was raised of whether authorisation is needed while that document does not appear on the Departament’s website.

The answer does not depend on that document. In education, the processing of students’ data is based on the public-interest mission, not on consent. This is laid down in Article 6(1)(e) of the General Data Protection Regulation and in the twenty-third additional provision of Spain’s Organic Law on Education, as amended by the LOMLOE, which allows schools to process the student data they need for their educational function, and explained by the Spanish Data Protection Agency (in Spanish). It applies throughout Spain; the Orientacions apply it to AI in Catalonia (OIA-02).

Clear guidelines follow from this. Families must be informed about which tool is used, with which account and for what. Their consent is not needed for students aged 14 to 18 to use the tools provided by the school or the education authority, although the Orientacions recommend collecting authorisation as a safeguard and offer a template in their annex 5. Authorisation is compulsory when the tool’s terms require it, for example if students have to open a personal account with a service that asks for parental permission for under-18s. For students under 14, the Orientacions state that they should not use general-purpose AI on their own, but that the teacher should operate it (OIA-01 and OIA-23); other regions set different criteria, which the paper will review in this Sunday’s report.

If a family objects, its decision affects its own child, not the group. The Regulation recognises the right to object (Article 21), which the Orientacions include in OIA-16, and their authorisation template already provides equivalent alternative activities for students who are not yet old enough, a solution that also works in this case. Stopping using the tool with the whole group is a teaching decision by the school, not a legal obligation.

Editor’s note: unlike other articles in this section, this one does not just summarise the conversation; the paragraphs on the rules were added in a manual review.

Vibe coding 7 November

Community app

A teacher replaces the school siren with a musical bell managed from a spreadsheet

David Cordones says that, with the help of generative AI, he built a school bell on a Raspberry Pi that staff change from Google Sheets.

The school musical bell, created by David Cordones, replaces the siren with melodies chosen by the teachers themselves. According to its author, it already runs daily at his school after a trial period.

The setup is modest: a Raspberry Pi connected by cable to the network and to the school’s public address system. A Google Sheets spreadsheet with drop-down menus lets those in charge choose which song plays at each period or break. The songs are MP3 files that are dragged into a shared folder on the local network.

The logic is handled by a Python script, which checks the calendar every minute and uses a local copy if the internet goes down, and a Google Apps Script bridge that updates the drop-down menus when a new MP3 appears. According to its author, AI helped to design the script and the background services, to write the bridge and to solve problems with permissions, audio and the network.

The author offers to share diagrams, code and a technical guide with anyone who wants to replicate it. For now the description comes from his message: there is no public project page and it has not been possible to check that it works.

Vibe coding 7 November

The repository's week

An auction of phrases, a guide to responsible vibe coding, chemistry, music and illustrated books: new in the repository

Since 27 October, the Vibe Coding Educativo resource repository has added sixteen resources: ten have already had their own article in the paper and six are presented here, two by Juan José de Haro, one by Felip Sarroca, one by FiQuiPedia and two by Samuel Soriano.

Every Saturday, the paper reviews the resources added during the week to the Vibe Coding Educativo Resource repository, the collection of openly licensed apps and materials created and shared by teachers in that community.

Presented here are six openly licensed resources the paper had not yet covered: a debate activity, a guide to publishing responsibly what is created with AI, two chemistry apps for formulation and stoichiometry, a set of music games and an illustrated-books workshop. They open in the browser and, except where noted for each one, require no account. Resources by the same person are grouped together.

Subasta de frases

Juan José de Haro · AGPL v3 (code) · CC BY-SA 4.0 (content)

Juan José de Haro has created a participatory activity in which the teacher prepares an auction and students join with a session code to bid on phrases. It has three modes: “Conocimiento”, where students bid on the statements they consider correct and defend their decision; “Valores y prioridades”, which shares a limited budget among the ideas that matter most to the group; and “Roles y conceptos”, where a correct match earns points and a wrong one loses them. The page states that it requires no registration and that the data is ephemeral. It is available in six languages, including Spanish, Catalan, Galician and Basque. AGPL v3 licence for the code and CC BY-SA 4.0 for the content.

Open the resource · Its entry in the repository

Vibe coding responsable

Juan José de Haro · AGPL-3.0 (code); CC-BY-SA-4.0 (content)

Juan José de Haro publishes a non-technical guide for teachers who share materials created with AI through vibe coding. According to its introduction, it grew out of materials that circulate without a licence or that send students' data to external services, and it proposes ten recommendations: correct content, data protection, accessibility, attribution, a declaration of AI use and an open licence, among others. It includes an infographic, an animation, downloadable instructions to give to the AI when creating, and a rubric (VCER) for evaluating a resource. It can be read on the web, there is a PDF version and it is available in five languages. According to its author, it is open to suggestions.

Open the resource · Its entry in the repository

Reactius i sobrants

Felip Sarroca i Gil · CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

Felip Sarroca i Gil has created, with AI assistance, an interactive laboratory in Catalan for understanding the limiting reactant, the excess reactant and leftovers; according to its author, it is designed for lower and upper secondary. It has three modes: “Aprèn”, “Practica” and “Repte”. In practice mode, students see the reaction with molecular models, count how many products form and how many reactants are left over, and can ask for a hint or the solution. It offers several types of activity, three difficulty levels and a scoreboard of attempts, correct answers and streak. According to the listing, it also includes a glossary, atomic masses, progress saved in the browser and a projector mode for the interactive whiteboard. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 licence.

Open the resource · Its entry in the repository

FoQa – Formulación Química Inorgánica

FiQuiPedia · AGPL v3 (code) · CC BY-SA 4.0 (content)

FiQuiPedia presents FoQa, a Spanish-language app for practising inorganic formulation and nomenclature according to the IUPAC 2005 rules. It generates tests on binary compounds, hydroxides, peroxides, oxoacids, oxysalts, acid salts, homoatomic species and ions, and offers a converter from formula to possible names. In the panel, families and rules are chosen, such as strictness with accents, and it can be exported to PDF; according to the listing, to SCORM as well. According to its author, it was made with DeepSeek and is designed to work as a Moodle activity and report the grade to the gradebook. The newspaper has already covered its counterpart for organic formulation, FoQo. AGPL v3 and CC BY-SA 4.0 licence.

Open the resource · Its entry in the repository

EduMúsic

Samuel Soriano · MIT

In EduMúsic, Samuel Soriano brings together a collection of music activities, listed in the entry as being for primary school, with an interface in Valencian and a language selector that also offers Spanish and English. They can be filtered by level (beginner, intermediate, advanced) and by tags. It includes games for catching notes on the stave, an instrument memory game, time-signature puzzles, rhythmic and melodic dictations, timbre recognition, a two-octave keyboard and rankings saved on the device itself. It also links to external resources, such as JClic or EduHoot. According to the listing, it is a progressive web app that can be installed. MIT licence.

Open the resource · Its entry in the repository

EduTicTac Llibres

Samuel Soriano · AGPL v3

Samuel Soriano is also behind EduTicTac Llibres, a workshop for creating illustrated books and comics with text, images and drawings, with the page in Spanish. The page lets users create or open a book without signing in; books are saved in the browser, so it is advisable to download a copy. It comes with a sample story and comic in Valencian. According to the listing, it uses eXeLearning and templates, is designed for primary, secondary and teacher training, and books are downloaded as .elpx files and can be published in a shared bank with a teacher account; students sign in with a class code and PIN, and their teacher approves the books. The same person created the block editor EduTicTac Blocs. AGPL v3 licence.

Institutions & Industry 7 November

Usage policy

Anthropic bans sustained, gratuitous abuse of its models in its usage policy

The update, published on 8 October 2026, takes effect on 12 November and will be enforced only in extreme cases.

Anthropic has added to its usage policy a ban on sustained, unnecessary abusive or cruel behaviour towards its models. According to the announcement, it will be enforced only in extreme cases, involving repeated cruelty with no apparent purpose, and it does not cover everyday frustration, disagreement, dark creative themes, or testing and research on the models.

The announcement says that Claude’s existing ability to end rare conversations with persistently abusive users on Claude.ai and Claude Code will remain the main enforcement mechanism. The update includes other changes, such as a section on deceptive activity and a rewording of the elections section. In ChatGPT-IA-edu the measure was read as a principle of caution, with no admission by the company that its models suffer; that reading comes from the group’s comments, because the announcement does not develop the question. It matters to teachers who use these tools, because it is a change to the terms of use.

Vibe coding 7 November

Community app

EDUmind MiClase, an open-source teacher’s notebook, keeps data on the device and syncs without its own server

Its author explained in the group how it syncs two devices and which features he says he has added to assess under the LOMLOE.

EDUmind MiClase is an open-source teacher’s notebook, created by Luis Vilela Acuña, with assessment by criterion and instrument under the LOMLOE. Its repository states that students’ data never leaves the teacher’s device and that it works offline. It is licensed under AGPL-3.0 or EUPL-1.2. EDUmind is the same initiative behind the Galician and Spanish dictation tools that the paper reported on 30 October.

Faced with a question about how to sync with Firebase, its author explained another route: a direct link between devices on the same wifi network, with no servers or external services. The repository confirms that syncing can be done over WebRTC, with no intermediate server, or through encrypted envelopes that the server cannot open. The author adds that the password is compared before anything is sent, that newer data is not overwritten by older, and that photo and video evidence is requested separately so as not to exhaust memory; these details come from his message.

According to the author, he has also added assessment of curricular adaptations and support measures, analysis of key competences and operational descriptors, and local analysis of Galician teaching plans in PDF; the repository does not detail this and it has not been checked. For those who code their own apps, it is an example of how to reduce data-protection risks.

Multimedia 7 November

AI video

Google Flow charges credits per second for Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash videos

Flow’s official help page sets out the cost of generating and editing clips with the model, which can already be chosen in the app, according to comments in the group.

Following the unveiling of Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash, which the paper reported on 6 November, it was noted in EduMedia-IAG that Google Flow lets users choose Omni 1.1 or Omni 1.2 and that each generation uses credits.

According to Flow’s official help page, generating or editing videos of up to 10 seconds costs 1 credit per second at 360p and 3 at 720p, and is open to all users. Clips of up to 15 seconds are reserved for Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra plans, at a higher cost for each second beyond 10. Without a subscription there are 50 daily credits that do not roll over, and with a subscription between 200 (Plus) and 25,000 (the $200 Ultra plan) a month. Google warns that costs may change.

A year ago 7 November 2025

From NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook

NotebookLM presentations, once impossible to tweak, can now be revised slide by slide

A year ago, anyone wanting an editable presentation had to leave NotebookLM. Today the tool, which no longer even goes by that name, makes slides, amends them on request and exports them to PowerPoint.

On 7 November 2025, in ChatGPT-IA-edu, someone asked whether NotebookLM could be made to use their own images in a presentation. The answer captured the limit at the time: NotebookLM made video presentations, which could not be modified once created, and anyone who wanted slides to present and tweak had to turn to Gemini.

Weeks later, slide decks arrived, as Google recalled on 16 December in “8 ways to make the most out of Slide Decks in NotebookLM”. On 20 March 2026, the Google Workspace Updates blog announced that users could send comments on style or content for each slide and that the presentation could be exported as PPTX as well as PDF.

On 16 July, the tool was renamed Gemini Notebook. And on 19 October Google explained in “How to shape your slides and videos with Gemini Notebook” that it suggests topics based on the sources and lets users review the plan before generating the slides or the video. The question from a year ago is no longer whether anything can be changed, but how much it is wise to leave in its hands.
